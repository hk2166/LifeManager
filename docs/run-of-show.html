Cold open — name the problem

Don't touch anything yet. Let the dashboard sit behind you.

Screen

Dashboard, Today tab — already open.

Say

"Our lives are split across Slack, WhatsApp, email, and calendar — and things fall through the cracks. The hard part was never reading all of it. It's remembering it. Life OS does the remembering."

10:20–1:0040s

Commitments & Waiting On — the hook

This is the feature judges recognize instantly. Don't rush it.

Screen

Commitments tab → source drawer → Waiting On tab.

Do
  1. Click Commitments
  2. Click the row Send revised offsite deck to Priya — the drawer opens the exact source email.
  3. Click Waiting On
Say

"It read this inbox and pulled out every promise. You told Priya you'd send the deck by Thursday — here's the exact sentence where you said it. And here's the inverse: everything you're waiting on from other people. Nobody builds this, and everybody needs it."

The moment

The voice memo files itself

Switch to the phone. Speak the two lines exactly — they're the validated ones; don't improvise on stage.

Screen

The phone (mirrored or on camera), then back to the dashboard.

Do
  1. Tap the center 🎤 mic — it starts recording on the first tap.
  2. Speak, then tap Stop:
    Say exactly"Dinner with Nina next Friday at 8 PM at the ramen place."
  3. It transcribes, classifies, and files it — Added to events.
  4. Tap 🎤 mic again:
    Say exactly"Call the dentist."
  5. It's not sure → a confirmation chip row appears. Tap task.
  6. Switch back to the dashboard — both items are already there.
Say

"Capture is one tap. It transcribed, classified, and filed it — and when it's genuinely unsure, it asks instead of guessing silently. The raw audio stays attached to whatever it creates."

52:40–3:0020s

The close — roadmap

Land the "moat" line and stop. Don't trail off.

Screen

Roadmap slide.

Do
  1. Switch to the roadmap tab.
Say

"Email and calendar this weekend. WhatsApp and Slack are the same pipeline — every inbox is just an adapter. The memory layer is the moat. That's Life OS."

If you run long

Cut in this order: beat 3 (brief), then beat 2 (ask).

Never cut beats 1 and 4 — the waiting-on tracker and the self-filing voice memo are the demo.

If the network drops

Switch to the fallback-recording tab and narrate over it. Keep the pace — don't apologize.

Ask (beat 2) slow? Keep talking through the "died in June" line; it catches up.
